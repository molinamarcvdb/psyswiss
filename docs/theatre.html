1 · The portfolio — what it must contain

Do this first
  • 2 headshots (neutral + character). Natural light is fine; no filters, no sunglasses, current look.
  • Showreel 1–3 min: best stage moments first, then one contrasting register (e.g. classical vs physical comedy). Phone-shot rehearsal footage beats nothing.
  • One-page stage CV: training, roles (role / play / director / venue / year), workshops, languages with honest levels, special skills (singing, dance, instruments, stage combat, clown…).
  • Reviews & proof: press quotes, festival selections, audience numbers — even small venues count.
  • Languages box, prominent: FR (level?) · ES native · DE · EN. Casting directors scan for this.
  • One contact line everywhere: email + phone + base city (Genève). No agent needed at this stage.

Format: one PDF + one page (even a simple site) holding reel + CV + contact. Update it after every production.

2 · Where beginners actually enter in Geneva

Geneva's scene runs on compagnies (small companies) hosted by mid-size venues — not on big-house auditions. The realistic doors, in order:

  1. Bureau des compagnies (Scènes du Grütli) — the city's front door for emerging companies: support, hosting and visibility. First email to send after the portfolio is ready.
  2. Résidences (Théâtre du Galpon) — rehearsal space + time to build a piece. Residencies are how unknown performers become known quantities.
  3. Open calls / appels à projets — Grütli's bureau, SSA's writing/project supports (incl. humour), and seasonal calls. Check the pages below monthly; deadlines cluster autumn/winter.
  4. Author-creator route (SSA, Lausanne) — if she writes her own material (multilingual solo piece!), the Société Suisse des Auteurs' fonds culturel funds writing and creations.
  5. City project funding (Ville de Genève) — soutien à la culture: project grants once a compagnie or collective exists. Long game, but the standard path to paid productions.

3 · Money, status & safety nets — the Swiss specifics

  • “Intermittent” has no legal meaning in Switzerland. The AVS/tax offices only distinguish salarié·e vs indépendant·e. Stage performers are normally salaried per engagement (each production = a fixed-term contract, even oral) — the employer pays social contributions.
  • But an intermittence safety net exists since 2003 (art. 13 al. 4–5 LACI): for cultural professions with frequent employer changes, contribution periods count double for the first 60 days of fixed-term contracts — easing access to unemployment insurance between gigs. Independent-only artists get no chômage: many performers mix both statuses legally.
  • AVS is due from the first franc in culture (no CHF 2,300 franchise) — tell tiny employers this; many don't know.
  • Get advice before signing: t. Professions du spectacle Suisse (union, model contracts, salary benchmarks) and Action Intermittence (social-security guidance for cultural workers).
  • Harassment prevention: t. runs the sector's prevention resources — worth knowing from day one.

Sources: Artists Take Action guide · t. intermittent·e ou indépendant·e · statut intermittent depuis 2003

4 · Why 4 languages is a genuine edge

  • French unlocks 90% of Romandie casting — keep levelling it; it's also the therapy-career language.
  • Spanish opens Spanish-language productions, Latin-American festival circuits, and surtitled work where the company needs a native speaker.
  • German opens touring into Suisse alémanique (bigger budgets, more venues) — most Romand performers can't.
  • English covers international/experimental companies and physical-theatre ensembles working in English.

Concrete move: a short multilingual solo piece (15–20 min) is the highest-ROI calling card — it turns “four languages” from a CV line into something programmers can book, and it's fundable via SSA/Ville de Genève calls.
